I'm a third-year B.Tech AIML student at Symbiosis Institute of Technology in Pune, and a research
and project intern at SCAAI, where I build end-to-end ML pipelines on an NDA-restricted laboratory
dataset — data cleaning, feature engineering, multi-output model training, and deployment.

The thing I keep circling back to is the part of ML that isn't modeling. Most of the projects on this
site started as a system that appeared to work and turned into a much longer argument about whether it
actually did. On LegalAI, dense retrieval had been silently running as BM25-only for an unknown stretch
because the embedding model was quietly 404ing — the feature "worked" the entire time. On the
e-commerce intelligence project, the predictive models scored suspiciously well, and chasing that
down surfaced data leakage serious enough that I dropped the predictive framing entirely rather than
ship something that wouldn't generalize. On the reconciliation agent, I had to walk back a change that
pushed recall up, because it was converting honest escalations into confident wrong answers.

That's why every case study here has a **What Broke** section, and why they're the honest ones. I'd
rather show the bug that cost me two days than a results table with no story behind it.

The pattern across most of what I build: use the cheap deterministic path first and route to a model
only for what's genuinely left over; keep the system provider-agnostic by construction rather than by
good intentions; and build the measurement before trusting the result. Right now I'm most interested
in self-hosted inference, retrieval and agent evaluation, and the unglamorous infrastructure that
makes any of it reproducible.
